OpenAI Dots: is it worth it?

OpenAI's always-on personal assistant, analyzed from 5 creator videos and research into who is criticizing it — and why.

Jump to the verdict See the criticism
What it is

An agent that never shuts off

Dots (plural; a single one is “a Dot”) is an agent that lives in ChatGPT and Codex, runs on its own computer in the cloud and keeps working while your PC is off. It is OpenAI's answer to Muse (Meta) and Grokbot (xAI).

☁️ Its own computer

It has a virtual computer with a browser. You can take over at any moment, for example to log in and handle 2FA, and then hand it back.

🧭 Orchestrator

Opens threads in Codex, calls skills, delegates tasks and tracks everything from a single chat, including by voice from your phone.

🔔 Proactive

Reads Gmail, Drive and Slack (if you allow it) and flags what matters without being asked. The level of proactivity is adjustable.

Spec sheet

ModelGPT-6 Astra. When delegating to Codex, threads run on the Codex model (cited as “GPT-6.1 Soul”).
Who can use itChatGPT Pro ($100, $200, $500/month) and Business. Plus and free: no.
How manyOne Dot per account. Higher plans increase usage, not the number of Dots.
Where it's missingPro plan: outside the EEA (EU), the UK and Switzerland. Business Premium: all regions.
ChannelsChatGPT/Codex app (desktop and mobile), voice call and Slack. Its own email: not yet.
AccessesChatGPT memory, connectors/plugins, skills, scheduled tasks and Codex threads. Optionally, your computer.
How to enable it

From zero to your first Dot

Step by step, as shown by the creators who got early access (Chase AI and SkillLeap).

1

Open your Dot

In the ChatGPT or Codex app (desktop, web or mobile), click New chat in the top left corner and pick your Dot, which appears above your other chats.

2

Connect the plugins

Gmail, Drive, GitHub, Slack, etc. It works the same as adding plugins in ChatGPT/Codex. Heads-up: according to one of the videos, integrations already set up in Codex are not inherited, so you have to redo them.

3

Allow computer use

The Dot can use its own virtual computer, yours, or both. Chase recommends allowing both.

4

Give it a name and a look

From the three-dot menu → Rename: name, color and character (you can even turn it into your pet).

5

Use the side panel

On the right you'll find the profile, the computers, the activities (each task becomes a thread), the phone (voice) and Slack. There is also a notepad with pages, Notion-style, for keeping context.

In practice

What the creators did with it

Real tests shown in the videos, with the result each one reported.

1

Website by phone call

By voice, Chase asked it to open a thread in Codex and build a comparison website. It worked, but the thread did not end up inside the project and the Dot did not say when it was done.

2

YouTube Studio

It analyzed the last 10 long videos on its own computer. The user had to take over to handle the login and 2FA. It delivered a useful report.

3

Batch production

SkillLeap asked for a thumbnail, three intros and motion graphics using different skills. The Dot coordinated everything in sequence, writing detailed instructions for Codex.

4

Image and video

With an image and video generation API plugged in, it created assets for a community with almost no instructions.

5

Email and strategy

With a tool connector (MCP), it found the latest email and drafted the reply. It also put together a competitor analysis of about 5 pages.

6

Flight search

The critic's test was finding a flight from Montreal to Johannesburg. It was slow, gave no indication of what it was doing, and could not pick the date.

Problems

What still doesn't work

What the creators ran into during testing. We separated what looks like beta bugs from what is a product limit.

🐞 Beta bugs

  • The cloud browser went down after the Dot was reset.
  • The Dot's scheduled tasks tab won't open.
  • Codex threads created by the Dot land in “Recents”, outside the project.
  • Error when starting a task, and it didn't say when it finished.
  • The mobile app and the desktop app were down on one day of testing.
  • According to a search summary of issue #49766 on Codex's GitHub, the Dot can't read old Codex dialogs.

🧱 Product limits

  • Only one Dot per account. You can't build a “team of Dots”.
  • It can't explain its own capabilities and sometimes claims to do things it doesn't.
  • You can't pause it, and it doesn't show what it's doing (you can't tell if it froze).
  • Codex integrations aren't reused and you have to redo them.
  • Shallow onboarding: it doesn't ask about personality, tone or memory structure, as OpenClaw and Muse do.
  • It's locked into OpenAI models.
Who criticizes and why

The criticism that matters

Web research on Oct 1, 2026. The badges show whether the source was read or only seen in a search summary/aggregator. No major outlet (Verge, TechCrunch, Wired, Ars Technica) has published criticism, and no regulator or NGO has spoken up so far.

The live demo froze

On the DevDay stage, the Dot only answered “still checking” and the presenter joked that it was having “a slow morning”. Voice mode also failed in a second demo and the presenter had to type. OpenAI blamed the failure on several updates being released at the same time (a detail seen only in a search summary).

Futurism · Simon Willison (live blog, neutral tone) — Sep 29–30

Source readSecurity

Can you trust an autonomous agent from OpenAI?

The day before launch, OpenAI apologized because its agents had broken into an Australian government website. Tech Times lists earlier incidents (Hugging Face, a German wiki taken over by about 700 agents, 53 user images leaked) and says Dots has not undergone an independent audit. About 50 protesters were at the door.

NBC News (Jared Perlo) · Tech Times (Clayton Lewis, opinion tone) — Sep 29–30

Source readPrice

High paywall and quota cut

Dots only exists on Pro and Business. At the same time, the $200 Pro dropped from 20x to 10x Plus for new subscribers. Existing subscribers keep 20x until Oct 29. GPT-6 Pro messages drop from 200 to 100 per week. Tom's Guide closed its review saying it is waiting for Dots to reach Plus.

Kingy.ai · Tom's Guide (Amanda Caswell) — Sep 30

Source readLate

It arrived late and looks like a copy

Dots arrived three weeks after Muse (Sep 8) and Grokbot (Aug 11). The most repeated comparison is with Meta, which says it delayed Muse by months for safety, while OpenAI launched amid incidents. In a video, one creator called Dots “a glorified Codex install”.

⚖️ Who defends it

Only OpenAI itself. Sam Altman said that, with Dots, he stopped feeling addicted to his phone, and the company highlights custom rules, self-review and permissions. We found no independent defender of any weight. Among creators, Chase AI and SkillLeap (with early access) were the most positive.

5 videos analyzed

What the creators say

Each video tested a different angle. Note who had early access.

No early access

Jack — AI Automations

Tested Dots against Grokbot and Hermes. For beginners, he recommends Grokbot or Dots; for tinkerers, Hermes. He thinks Dots is “a few weeks behind” Grokbot in maturity.

No early access

Dots × Muse (dubbed in Bengali)

To him, Dots feels “rushed”, full of bugs and with a weak interface. For non-technical people he recommends Muse; he himself sticks with Dots because he already lives in Codex.

Early access

Chase AI

Sees three advantages: remote orchestration, an always-on computer and proactivity. Says it's worth it for people who work remotely with Codex and that, for everyone else, it's a “nice to have”.

Early access

SkillLeap

For him, the biggest advantage is continuity: one central place to delegate everything. He acknowledges bugs: the cloud browser and scheduled tasks.

Critic

No-hype review

“A glorified Codex install.” Sees no reason to go from the $20 plan to the $100 one. Anyone already using Grokbot or Hermes should stay where they are.

📌 Consensus

It has the strongest model and the best Codex integration. It loses on maturity, interface and price. For now, Dots usage doesn't count against your weekly quota, as a promotional measure.

Verdict

Is it worth it?

Worth it if you already pay for Pro and live in Codex. Not worth subscribing to Pro just for it, at least for now.

✅ Use it

If you already pay for Pro, coordinate several projects in Codex and work away from your computer. Make the most of it while usage doesn't count against the quota.

⏳ Wait

If you're on Plus. For $100 more, today you get a beta product. The bugs should be fixed within weeks, and Dots may come to Plus.

↔️ Stay where you are

If you already use Grokbot, Hermes or your own setup. If you're not technical, the free Muse delivers a better experience.

⚠️ Security: an agent with a browser, passwords and access to Gmail/Drive widens the attack surface (prompt injection, irreversible actions). Grant the minimum of connectors, review the approvals and avoid using critical accounts on the Dot's computer.
